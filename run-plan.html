How this plan works

The week

  • Sun long run · Mon Gym A (optional easy run from week 3) · Tue easy run · Wed off · Thu workout · Fri Gym B · Sat easy run
  • Saturday is a run day, not a gym day, so your legs and hips start every long run fresh.

4 or 5 days?

Five days still means one workout. The 5th run is an easy 3–4 miles on Monday. Your engine is already ahead of your legs (your heart rate stayed low all race), so a second hard day adds injury risk without fixing what limited you. When the spring half block starts, the long run picks up steady miles and becomes your second quality day.

Strength

  • Weeks 1–2: re-entry. 2 sets, light, no soreness the next day.
  • Weeks 3–9: build. 3 sets; the last 2 reps of each set are hard but clean.
  • Weeks 10–16: strength. Heavier main lifts at lower reps, plus pogo hops and lateral bounds.
  • Add weight once you hit the top of the rep range on every set.
  • Cutback weeks (5, 9, 13, 16): 2 sets of everything, same weights.
  • Upper body is gym only. Pair the moves to stay inside 45–60 minutes.

Your scoreboard

On each long run, note the mile where your hips first speak up. Before Columbus it was miles 7–11. That number moving later is how you'll know the strength work is paying off.

Pain rules

  • Dull and steady: keep going.
  • Building mile over mile: slow to recovery pace or cut the run short.
  • Sharp, makes you limp, or the outside of your knee hurts on stairs: stop running and see a PT.
